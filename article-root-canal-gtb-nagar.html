Root canal treatment in Delhi: what it actually costs, and whether it hurts

By Dr. Animesh Agarwal · Delhi Dental Cosmetics, GTB Nagar · 6 min read

A root canal has a reputation it did nothing to earn. Most patients who sit in the chair expecting pain leave surprised by how ordinary the visit felt. The pain they remember happened before the treatment, when the nerve inside the tooth was infected.

What a root canal actually is

Inside every tooth is a soft core of nerve and blood vessels. When decay goes deep, or a tooth cracks, bacteria reach that core and it gets infected. A root canal cleans that infected space out, disinfects it, and seals it. The tooth stays in your mouth. The infection leaves.

The alternative is extraction. And an extracted tooth always asks for a replacement later, an implant or a bridge, which costs more than the root canal would have.

Does it hurt?

Here is the honest version. The dentist numbs the tooth first, properly, and waits for the numbness to work. What you feel during the procedure is pressure and vibration, not sharp pain. The tooth can stay tender for a few days afterwards while the tissue around it settles. That soreness is normal and it fades.

Patients at our clinic routinely describe the RCT itself as the easier part. The sleepless night before it was the hard part.

How many visits does it take?

A straightforward root canal can finish in a single sitting. More involved cases, where the infection has spread to the bone or the tooth has complicated roots, need two or three visits with a dressing left inside between them. Anyone who promises you an exact visit count before looking at your X-ray is guessing. The X-ray decides, not the sales pitch.

What does it cost in Delhi?

Root canal treatment across Delhi typically runs between about 3,000 and 9,000 rupees for the RCT itself, depending on which tooth it is and how complex the roots are. Front teeth are simpler. Molars carry three and four canals and take longer. A crown placed afterwards usually adds a few thousand more, and zirconia crowns sit at the higher end of that range.

Two pieces of advice worth more than any price comparison. First, ask what the total will be including the crown, before the first visit starts. Second, be suspicious of quotes far below the market range, because the difference usually shows up later in materials or in work that has to be redone.

When to go, and when to wait

Go when a tooth hurts on its own without any trigger, when cold or hot pain lingers for more than a few seconds, when a tooth has visibly darkened, or when a small pimple appears on the gum near it. That last one is an abscess draining, and it needs treatment soon.

Do not wait for the pain to become unbearable. Teeth do not heal themselves, and an infection that reaches the bone turns a straightforward RCT into a bigger procedure.

One last thing

If a dentist tells you a painful tooth cannot be saved and must come out, ask why, and ask for the X-ray to be shown to you. In our experience most painful teeth can be saved when the patient arrives in time. Saving a tooth is almost always the better long-term answer.
