Crowns and bridges: which material actually lasts, and what it should cost

By Dr. Animesh Agarwal · 15 Oct 2026 · Crowns & Bridges

A crown is the last stop for a tooth that has lost too much structure to a root canal, a large cavity or a crack. It wraps the remaining tooth in a hard shell so chewing forces cannot split it. A bridge does the same job for a missing tooth, suspending a false tooth between two crowned neighbours. The materials you choose decide how the crown looks, how long it lasts and what it costs.

The materials, honestly compared

MaterialBest forWatch out for
ZirconiaStrength and looks together; the current standard for back teeth and full-mouth workCosts more; quality varies a lot between labs
Porcelain fused to metal (PFM)Budget work on back teethA grey line at the gumline after a few years; can chip
All-ceramic / E-maxFront teeth, where looks matter mostLess strong than zirconia for heavy grinders
Metal / gold alloyDeep grinders, second molars where nobody looksColour. Nothing else. Gold is an excellent material

For most of our patients the answer is zirconia. One of our patients from Shamli had twenty-eight zirconia crowns placed across both arches and writes, two years on, that it is the best money he has spent. Full-arch zirconia is serious work and the per-unit cost reflects it.

What crowns cost in Delhi

As a market range, PFM crowns typically run about 3,000 to 6,000 rupees per unit, all-ceramic about 6,000 to 12,000, and zirconia about 8,000 to 18,000 depending on the lab and the brand of the block used. A three-unit bridge is priced as three units. Anything quoted dramatically below these ranges is worth a question about which lab is making it, because the lab matters as much as the dentist.

The part nobody tells you

The crown is only as good as the tooth under it and the fit on top of it. A crown on an unfilled root canal is a failure waiting for a date. A crown with an open margin leaks bacteria under itself and the tooth dies silently beneath it. When we deliver a crown we check the margins, the contact points and the bite, and we tell patients to return if anything feels high after a week. A high crown is not something to get used to; it is something to adjust.

How long should it last?

A well-made zirconia crown on a healthy foundation routinely serves fifteen to twenty years or more. The main killers are decay at the margin from poor cleaning and night grinding without a guard. If you grind, say so before the crown is made; a night guard is far cheaper than a redo.
