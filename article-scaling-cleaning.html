Scaling and polishing: the least glamorous visit that saves the most teeth

By Dr. Animesh Agarwal · 15 Oct 2026 · Prevention

Nobody writes reviews about a cleaning. They write reviews about implants and smile makeovers. And yet the unglamorous scaling visit, done twice a year, is the reason many people never need the glamorous work at all. Here is what the appointment actually involves and what it is worth.

What scaling removes that brushing cannot

Plaque is the soft film that brushing removes. Left for more than a couple of days, it hardens into tartar, also called calculus, which bonds to the tooth like limescale to a kettle. At that point your brush is finished; the deposit comes off only with ultrasonic instruments. Tartar below the gumline is the main driver of gum disease and of the bone loss that follows it.

What the appointment involves

  • Ultrasonic scaling: vibrations and a water spray break the tartar off in pieces. It feels like tapping, not drilling.
  • Hand instruments for the fine deposits between the teeth.
  • Polishing with a gritty paste, which removes tea, coffee and tobacco stains and leaves the surface too smooth for plaque to grip easily.
  • A check of every pocket depth, so gum problems are caught at the reversible stage.

The whole thing takes thirty to forty minutes. For most patients, mildly sensitive teeth for a day is the whole story. Heavier deposits mean heavier cleaning and slightly more soreness, which is itself a useful lesson about the six-month interval.

The myths, cleared up

"Scaling makes teeth loose." Teeth that feel loose after scaling were being held in place by the tartar, which is like a splint made of disease. What felt like firmness was the problem itself. "Scaling creates gaps." The gaps were already there, filled with swollen gum tissue that shrinks once the infection clears. "It damages enamel." Ultrasonic tips work on the mineral deposits; the enamel underneath is harder than the deposit and is not touched.

How often, and what it costs

Once every six months for most people; every three to four months for patients with a history of gum disease, diabetes or heavy staining. In Delhi a full scaling and polishing typically runs about 800 to 2,500 rupees depending on the clinic and how much work the deposits need. Compare that with the cost of a single implant, and the arithmetic makes itself.

Bleeding gums, morning bad breath, or stains you can feel with your tongue? Book a scaling visit. Your future self, the one who never needs a flap surgery or an implant, will thank you for it.
