Tooth pain at night: what it means and what to do about it tonight

By Dr. Animesh Agarwal · 15 Oct 2026 · Dental Emergency

Tooth pain that wakes you up at 2 AM is in a different category from the twinge you feel while chewing. Pain that lies down with you, throbs in waves, and refuses painkillers usually means the nerve inside the tooth is inflamed or infected. That is not a situation to ride out.

What different pains usually mean

Matching the pain to its cause helps you judge the urgency:

  • Sensitivity to cold that fades in seconds. Enamel or a small cavity. See a dentist this week, no emergency.
  • Pain on chewing, sore to bite on. A cracked tooth or a high filling. Days, not weeks.
  • Lingering ache after hot or cold. The nerve is irritated. Get seen within a day or two.
  • Throbbing pain that keeps you awake, worse lying down. The nerve is dying or infected. This needs treatment now.
  • Swelling of the face or jaw, fever. An abscess is spreading. This is the same-night situation.

Why night pain is worse

When you lie down, blood pressure to the head increases, and pressure inside an inflamed tooth rises with it. That is why the same tooth that grumbles all day screams at night. The pain pattern itself is diagnostic information; telling your dentist exactly when it hurts helps them find the tooth faster.

What actually helps tonight

Take the painkiller you normally use, at the labelled dose. Rinse with warm salt water if the gum around the tooth is swollen. Keep your head raised on an extra pillow, which genuinely lowers the pressure. Do not place aspirin directly on the gum; it burns the tissue. Do not apply heat to a swollen face; it can push the infection outward.

Antibiotics left over from an old prescription are not a plan. They mask the pain while the infection keeps hollowing the bone, and the tooth that could have been saved by a root canal on Monday may be unsalvageable by Friday.

What the dentist will do

An X-ray first, to see the roots and the bone. If the nerve is involved, opening the tooth to relieve the pressure, a drain if there is an abscess, and a treatment plan from there. Most patients with night pain walk out of the first visit with the pain dramatically reduced, because relieving the pressure inside the tooth is relief that no tablet can match.
